Cash Usage Database

How much of everyday consumer spending is still paid in cash? The database brings together cash shares in the number and value of consumer payments, taken from payment diaries and surveys run by central banks and other financial institutions.

About the database

The Cash Usage Database collects estimates of how much consumers pay in cash, country by country and year by year. It is a compilation of published results from payment diary studies and other surveys of consumer payment behaviour, most of them run by central banks. It is free to use for research, policy work, teaching and journalism. The dataset can be downloaded from Mendeley Data (DOI 10.17632/529hmx9pk6.3). If you use the data, please cite the paper below.

Variables

cash_usage_volume
Share of cash in the number of consumer payments. Stored as a fraction (0–1) in the downloadable file; shown as a percentage on this site.
cash_usage_value
Share of cash in the value of consumer payments. Stored as a fraction (0–1) in the downloadable file; shown as a percentage on this site. Missing where the source reports only the number share.
study_year
Year in which the survey data were collected, not the year of publication.
institution, source
Who ran the survey, and the publication the figures are taken from. The link points to that publication.

Comparability

  • Levels are more comparable within a country over time than across countries. Surveys differ in what counts as a consumer payment (point of sale only, or also bills, online purchases and person-to-person transfers), in age range and in season.
  • Most figures come from payment diaries, in which respondents record every payment over a few days. Some come from recall surveys, where respondents report their payments over a past period from memory.

Source selection

  • One main series per country, chosen for consistency over time. Where the national central bank runs its own regular survey (for example Germany, the Netherlands, Canada and the United States), that series is used; for the other euro-area countries, the ECB studies (Esselink and Hernández, 2017; SPACE 2019–2024).
  • Parallel surveys for the same country and year are not added.
  • Where the source changes over time, the series has a break. Austria: OeNB surveys up to 2016 and for 2025, ECB SPACE for 2019–2024.
  • Studies by independent researchers are used only where no central-bank survey exists for that country and year, and only if the sample is nationally representative.
  • Administrative data (Hungary, marked ‡; Croatia, marked ‖) are used where they give a longer consistent series than the available surveys; for Croatia they replace the single ECB SPACE 2024 observation.

Considered but not included

  • Hungary 2010: a household payment diary by the Magyar Nemzeti Bank, Hungary's central bank (Takács, 2011) puts cash at 78% of the number and 50% of the value of payments. Not included because the Hungarian series uses online cash register data, and the two methods are not comparable.
